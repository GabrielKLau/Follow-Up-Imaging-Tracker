    Method

    A law or bill is counted only if it explicitly requires insurance coverage of follow-up imaging after an abnormal screening finding without patient cost-sharing. Measures that cover screening only, permit cost-sharing, or concern outreach, research or education are excluded. A state whose only law covers supplemental screening (for dense breasts or elevated risk) without follow-up imaging is shown in blue and earns no FIPI points. A mandate that reaches only Medicaid or public-employee plans is shown as a partial mandate (striped green) and earns no FIPI points. These are the eligibility criteria of the Follow-up Imaging Policy Index (FIPI) study presented at RSNA 2026.

    The starting point is the study's manual review of enacted laws (). The tracker then keeps the picture current: legislation is searched daily in full text through the LegiScan API, screened against the criteria, and reviewed by a person before it appears. Newly enacted laws turn a state green and update its FIPI score. Status, votes and sponsors update automatically, and every update links to the official legislature record.

    This tracker is a research tool, not legal advice. Coverage rules can depend on plan type and effective date; read the linked source.
